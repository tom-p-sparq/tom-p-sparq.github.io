Lantana camara

  • Introduced to India by the EIC
  • Taken over 30% of prime elephant habitat, invaded over 50% of tiger reserves
  • Climate change will accelerate its spread
  • Reduces biodiversity: toxic to animals; outcompetes native plants
  • Elephants are left with nothing to eat
  • Extremely difficult to remove; the estimated cost of removal throughout India is c.£150bn

The Lantana Collective

https://www.thelantanacollective.org/

Problem Statement

  • There are limited resources to help identify and monitor the spread of Lantana
  • No one is mapping and monitoring at a global scale to understand the true extent of the issue and share learnings on how to manage it
  • At a local level the resources are not available to programmatically identify lantana from drone images and therefore it requires manual and time-consuming human review
  • Satellite data is not possible for users to interpret with the human eye as it is not visually engaging and understandable

Quantification: training data

We used a weighted mean of the F1 score and binary cross-entropy (at 0.5 threshold) as the loss function when fitting.

\[ \mathrm{F1} = \frac{2}{\mathrm{recall}^{-1} + \mathrm{precision}^{-1}} = \frac{2TP}{2TP + FP + FN} \]

Conclusion

A simple, time-boxed investigation has produced a model artefact and training pipeline that demonstrates signal.

Quantified inference on unseen data has shown that there is room for improvement in the quantitative match between predictions and labelled data.

  • What quality is high enough for our purposes?
  • How well would two experts match in their labelling? Can we achieve that level of agreement?
  • What would happen if a human validated and fed back model predictions?

What we’d like to do

  • Investigate different model architectures (made easy through the deep learning library we’re using)
  • Make sure the loss function is appropriate for our purposes
  • Investigate different data augmentation techniques
  • Use more than one training image!
  • Investigate different training hyperparameters and algorithm variants (e.g. learning rate scheduling, layer freezing/unfreezing)
  • Make use of cloud computing to make training on more data feasible
  • Use a less naive inference pipeline
